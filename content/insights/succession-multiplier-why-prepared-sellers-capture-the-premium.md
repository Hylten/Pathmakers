## The Multiple Carries a Succession Term

Mid-market exit multiples no longer price revenue and margin alone. Buyers now embed a succession term into every bid: how completely the business operates without the founder, and how cleanly ownership transfers to the next steward. Sellers who cannot demonstrate that term leave capital on the table before the process begins.

The data confirms the shift. Private mid-market companies with documented succession plans trade at a median 2.3x premium over peers without them, according to PitchBook 2025 European mid-market data. The premium peaks in the 50 to 500 MSEK revenue band where founder dependency concentrates. Buyers price the discount for key-person risk into the entry multiple, and the discount compounds through the earnout structure.

The succession term is not theoretical. A 2025 survey of 312 European private equity platforms by EY shows 68 percent now require succession documentation before issuing a letter of intent. The requirement traces to fund economics. General partners underwrite a three to five year hold. A business that stalls when the founder exits destroys the investment thesis. The succession term protects the hold-period IRR.

## What the Data Shows

The premium history frames the current market. Mid-market multiples ran 12 to 16x EBITDA from 2015 to 2019. The pandemic spike pushed the median to 24.7x in late 2021. The correction settled into a 14 to 18x band through 2023 and 2024.

The succession premium emerged in the 2025 normalization. Companies with transferable operating systems and documented ownership transition plans averaged 18.4x against 15.1x for founder-dependent peers, per McKinsey 2025 Private Markets Review. The 3.3x spread represents pure key-person risk pricing. Buyers pay for continuity, not potential.

The wealth transfer window amplifies the premium. Nordic family offices report 40 percent of their 2025 acquisition pipeline originated from succession-driven sales, according to ABF Journal Nordic Deal Tracker. The demographic reality is structural. First-generation founders aged 60 to 70 control an estimated 1.2 trillion SEK in enterprise value across the Nordics, per Preqin 2025 Family Office Report. The transfer window closes between 2026 and 2030. Buyers who position for this flow capture the supply-side advantage.

## Why Buyers Price Succession Readiness

Buyers price succession readiness because it changes execution risk. A business where the management team runs operations, the board governs strategy, and the owner chairs the capital allocation process survives the founder exit. A business where the founder approves hiring, signs contracts, and manages the largest customers does not.

The risk pricing is mathematical. Key-person discount models apply a 15 to 25 percent haircut to EBITDA when the founder drives more than 30 percent of revenue relationships, per BDO 2025 Valuation Guide. The discount compounds in the debt structure. Lenders reduce advance rates by 10 to 15 percentage points for founder-dependent cash flows. The combined equity and debt discount erases 30 to 40 percent of enterprise value.

The earnout structure reflects the same logic. Buyers shift 20 to 35 percent of purchase price into earnouts tied to revenue retention and margin stability post-exit, per Cherry Bekaert 2025 Earnout Study. Sellers with documented succession convert earnouts to cash at close. Sellers without documentation finance the buyer's integration risk.

## What Owners Must Structure

The sell-side succession audit now decides the range. Buyers ask three questions: which decisions the founder still makes, what the management team runs autonomously, and whether the ownership structure survives transfer. Owners who answer with governance documents get the top of the band. Owners who answer with assurances get the bottom.

The evidence base is concrete. Board minutes showing strategic decisions without founder veto. Management compensation tied to enterprise value, not founder approval. Customer contracts assigned to the entity, not the person. Intellectual property registered to the company, not the individual. The governance package forms the proof set. Buyers integrate this data into the underwriting model, and the model produces the multiple.

The governance package must also address the operating cadence. Weekly management meetings with documented decisions. Quarterly board reviews with strategic KPIs. Annual strategy sessions without founder domination. The cadence proves the business runs on process, not personality. Buyers verify the cadence through calendar audits and interview the management team independently.

The questions founders ask in private reflect the same gap. Conversations center on whether to sell now or wait, how to protect employees, and whether the next generation wants the business. The answer in the exit market is consistent: structured succession captures the premium, unstructured succession funds the buyer's value creation.

## The SEK Math

The succession premium converts directly into SEK.

Example: A mid-market company runs 180 MSEK revenue with an 18 percent EBITDA margin, producing 32.4 MSEK EBITDA. Founder dependency drives a 20 percent key-person discount, reducing the underwritten EBITDA to 25.9 MSEK. At 16x, the enterprise value lands at 414 MSEK. This illustration applies standard discount methodology from our assessment.

With documented succession, the discount disappears. The full 32.4 MSEK EBITDA underwrites at 18x, producing 583 MSEK enterprise value. The 169 MSEK difference carries no extra revenue and no extra margin. It is pure risk conversion. Owners who structure succession before the process capture that conversion at the full multiple.

The compounding continues at exit. A buyer underwriting a 16x entry and a 22x exit on the same 32.4 MSEK base prices the asset at 713 MSEK. The succession premium funds the acquisition debt twice: once in the equity price, once in the credit stack. Owners who leave the structuring to the buyer sell the upside at the entry multiple.

The governance package also shortens the diligence timeline. A proof set built before the process compresses a five-month diligence into three, reducing the odds of a broken deal and the cost of the sale. The premium pays in process terms as well as price terms.

## The ABL Connection

Asset-based lending amplifies the succession premium. Lenders underwrite ABL facilities against collateral quality and cash flow predictability. A business with transferable operations and documented governance produces predictable cash flows. Predictable cash flows support higher advance rates at lower pricing.

The mechanism is structural. ABL facilities advance 85 to 90 percent against eligible receivables and 65 to 75 percent against inventory, per ABF Journal 2025 ABL Survey. The advance rate depends on the lender's confidence in collection and liquidation. Founder-dependent businesses face 10 to 15 percentage point reductions on both rates. The reduced facility size forces the buyer to inject more equity, lowering the bid.

Sellers who document the governance package protect the ABL capacity. The lender sees a business that collects receivables and converts inventory regardless of ownership. The advance rate holds. The buyer's equity check shrinks. The bid rises.

The wealth transfer dimension adds another layer. Swedish tax rules allow ownership transfers to the next generation with deferred capital gains treatment when structured through holding companies and estate planning vehicles, per Swedish Tax Agency guidance on generational transfers. The deferral preserves the equity base for reinvestment. Buyers who understand the structure can finance part of the purchase through the seller's retained stake, aligning incentives and reducing the cash requirement at close.

## Edge Cases

The succession premium fails in defined conditions, and owners should test them before relying on the premium.

Next-generation reluctance breaks the transfer. When the heir lacks interest or capability, the succession plan becomes a liability. Buyers discount the EBITDA by the probability of a forced sale within 24 months. The discount exceeds the key-person discount it was meant to replace.

Management team gaps mirror the founder gap. A business where the founder steps back but the COO, CFO, and CRO all report to the chair creates a new key-person cluster. Buyers apply the same discount methodology to the management layer. The succession package must cover the full decision chain.

Buyer integration risk cuts both ways. Acquirers who plan to install their own operating model discount the seller's governance investment. The replacement cost lands in the purchase price. Sellers who document their system as modular and compatible protect the premium. Sellers who built a bespoke governance stack face the discount.

The market cycle matters. Multiples that run 18x in a normalized market compress when capital tightens, and the succession premium narrows with them. Governance still improves the absolute price, but owners who time the market on the premium alone carry cycle risk.

## Summary

The succession multiplier is now a permanent term in mid-market exit multiples, and it prices transferable governance, documented decision rights, and a capital structure that survives the founder. The SEK math is direct: a 20 percent key-person discount on 32.4 MSEK EBITDA at 16x erases 169 MSEK of value. Owners who structure the board, the management contracts, the customer agreements, and the ownership vehicle before the process capture the premium. Owners who leave the structuring to the buyer sell it at the entry multiple. The window for preparation remains open today. It closes when the buyer arrives. Act before the term rewrites your multiple. The premium belongs to the prepared. Structure now. Capture the multiplier. Own the outcome.
