The seller who restructures the balance sheet before the auction sells a different company than the seller who lets the lender dictate the terms. The distinction shows up most clearly in asset-based lending. Per ABF Journal's mid-2026 market analysis, the first half of 2026 delivered a sharp reversal of fortune for cash-flow-based direct lending, with ABL emerging as the primary beneficiary as private credit recalibrates in the mid-market.

The scale is no longer niche. Per Lexology's Global Asset-Based Lending Guide 2026, the global ABL market surpassed the 1 trillion USD mark during 2026, roughly 11 trillion SEK at current rates. Fortune Business Insights projects the market growing from 937.13 billion USD in 2026 to 2,370.49 billion USD by 2034, a compound annual growth rate of 12.30 percent. The quiet lending product has become a structural feature of the mid-market capital stack.

## Why the Share Is Shifting

Cash-flow lending prices against historical EBITDA. Per CFA UK's analysis of asset-based lending in mid-market value creation, that model requires a clean, backwards-looking story to offer competitive terms. A company with lumpy earnings, a heavy order book or a seasonal profile fails that test. ABL prices against collateral: receivables, inventory, machinery and real estate, with borrowing bases that track the assets rather than the earnings narrative.

The private credit recalibration accelerates the shift. Direct lenders that tightened their cash-flow books in 2025 and early 2026 left a financing gap for exactly the companies their models could not serve. ABL stepped into the gap because the collateral was always there. The result is a two-track mid-market: cash-flow facilities for the clean earners, asset-based facilities for the asset-heavy operators, and the second track is growing faster.

## The Pre-Exit Balance Sheet

The seller preparing for exit has a three-year window to make the balance sheet legible. The ABL decision belongs inside that window, because it changes what the buyer sees. A company funded with an asset-based facility shows a borrowing base tied to real assets, predictable availability and a lender that understands the collateral. A company funded with an overextended cash-flow facility shows covenant pressure and a lender that is watching the earnings slide.

The leverage effect works in the seller's favour when it is quiet. The ABL line provides liquidity for the investments that raise the multiple: the ERP implementation, the sales team expansion, the working capital to take the large contract. The buyer prices the company on the earnings the line made possible, not on the line itself. The debt does the work and disappears from the narrative.

## The QoE Connection

The quality of earnings analysis meets the ABL structure at the working capital line. A sell-side QoE tests whether the working capital position supports the reported earnings. The seller who managed receivables through a disciplined ABL facility has the documentation: aging schedules, borrowing base certificates, collection history. The seller who financed growth through stretched payables has the opposite, a working capital position that the buyer's diligence will haircut.

The preparation logic is the same as the QoE logic. The buyer's discount is a function of the seller's information gap. Every document the seller can produce before the process, the borrowing base certificate, the collateral appraisal, the aging analysis, shortens the buyer's diligence and protects the multiple.

## The Timing Window

ABL structures take time to optimise. The borrowing base must be sized, the collateral appraised and the reporting package installed, and lenders want to see several clean reporting periods before they underwrite the full line. A seller who starts the ABL conversation eighteen months before market has the reporting history. A seller who starts it at teaser stage accepts whatever availability the lender offers in a hurry.

The market timing supports the early start. With private credit recalibrating and ABL capturing share, the product's terms are improving for borrowers. Lenders are competing for asset-rich mid-market companies, and competition shows up in advance rates, covenants and pricing. The window is open now, and like every window in the exit process, it closes when the process starts.

## The Edge Case

ABL is not the answer for every seller. A service company with no receivables concentration and no hard assets gains nothing from a borrowing base. A company whose receivables are concentrated in two customers will find the advance rate punishing. The structure rewards asset breadth and customer diversification, and the seller should test both before committing the balance sheet. The discipline, as in all exit preparation, is to match the instrument to the actual collateral, not to the trend.

## Summary

Asset-based lending has become a structural feature of the mid-market, with the global market past 1 trillion USD and private credit's recalibration pushing borrowers toward collateral-based structures. For the seller, the ABL decision is an exit preparation decision: it funds the investments that raise the multiple, produces the documentation that protects the QoE, and must be started early enough to build a clean reporting history. The quiet leverage works when it is arranged before the process, and it is priced into the exit either way.
