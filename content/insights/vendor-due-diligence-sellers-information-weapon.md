The exit process is an information contest. The buyer runs the diligence, controls the data room, and prices the risk. The seller answers questions and waits for the multiple. The asymmetry is structural, and the seller loses value in every round of questions the buyer must ask.

Vendor due diligence inverts the contest. The seller commissions the diligence, controls the narrative, and prices the risk before the buyer arrives. The data room becomes a weapon instead of a vulnerability.

## The Asymmetry That Costs Multiples

Buyers discount uncertainty. Every open question in diligence becomes a reserve in the purchase price. A customer concentration issue discovered by the buyer becomes a 0.5 multiple deduction. The same issue disclosed with a mitigation plan becomes a footnote.

The buyer's process is designed to find problems. The buyer's advisors are paid to test the seller's claims, and the testing produces findings that justify a lower price. The seller who reacts to the process accepts the buyer's framing of every issue.

Vendor due diligence changes the framing. The seller identifies the issues first, quantifies them, and presents the mitigation. The buyer inherits a data room where the problems are already solved, and the price conversation starts from the resolved state.

## What VDD Actually Covers

A vendor due diligence package mirrors the buyer's work. Financial diligence verifies the EBITDA bridge, the quality of earnings, and the normalized cost base. Commercial diligence documents the customer concentration, the pipeline, and the market position. Legal and tax diligence closes the structural gaps.

The difference is the posture. The buyer's diligence hunts for downside. The vendor's diligence builds the evidence base that supports the price. The same documents serve both purposes, but the seller controls which findings carry the emphasis.

The package must be built to the standard a professional buyer applies. A data room with unaudited numbers and missing contracts invites the buyer's team to write the findings. A data room with audited accounts, signed contracts, and a documented customer ledger invites the buyer's team to confirm the thesis.

## The Multiple Effect

The multiple is a function of perceived risk. The buyer prices the business on the certainty of its future cash flows. Vendor due diligence raises the certainty by removing the unknown.

The effect shows in the term sheet. A seller with a clean VDD package negotiates from a narrower discount range, because the buyer's diligence findings confirm the vendor's disclosures instead of discovering new ones. The process shortens, the exclusivity holds, and the auction dynamics stay in the seller's favor.

The preparation also protects the seller at closing. Working capital adjustments, indemnity caps, and earn-out structures are all negotiated against the data room. A seller whose numbers hold up under buyer diligence negotiates tighter caps and cleaner adjustments.

## The Timing Question

Vendor due diligence must be built before the process, not during it. A seller who commissions VDD at the start of the buyer's diligence has already conceded the timing advantage. The package exists to preempt the buyer's findings, and preemption requires completion before the buyer's team engages.

The build takes a quarter, not a week. The financials must be audited, the contracts gathered, the customer data verified, and the legal structure cleaned. The seller who starts twelve months before the exit builds the package at leisure. The seller who starts at the term sheet builds it under fire.

The prepared seller doctrine applies to the data room exactly as it applies to the business. The seller who has already answered the questions controls the conversation. The seller who answers them for the first time in the process surrenders the narrative.

## The Strategic Disclosure

Vendor due diligence includes strategic disclosure. The seller decides what the buyer learns, in what order, and with what framing. This is the information weapon in its purest form.

A customer concentration issue is disclosed with the retention history, the contract terms, and the expansion pipeline. A key-person risk is disclosed with the succession plan and the non-compete coverage. The buyer receives the issue and the solution in one package, and the price conversation moves past it.

The disclosure strategy also filters buyers. A serious buyer engages with the package, validates it, and moves to terms. A tire-kicker uses the data room to build a competing thesis. The prepared seller's package performs the qualification function that brokers perform by default.

## The Cost-Benefit

Vendor due diligence costs money at the worst possible time, before the exit check clears. The cost is a fraction of the value it protects. A package that shifts the multiple by 0.3 on a 100 MSEK business moves 30 MSEK of value.

The cost-benefit is stronger than it appears, because the package also shortens the process. A shorter process means less management distraction, fewer weeks of uncertainty, and a higher probability the deal closes. The seller pays for the package once and collects on it in the multiple, the timeline, and the closing rate.

The alternative cost is silent. The seller who skips VDD accepts the buyer's diligence as the sole source of truth, and the buyer's diligence is not designed to maximize the seller's price.

## The Execution Standard

The package is only as good as its standard. The seller's advisors must build to the level the buyer's advisors will test. A half-built data room is worse than none, because it signals the seller has something to hide.

The execution standard includes the operational readiness behind the documents. The management team must be prepared for the buyer's sessions. The financial model must reconcile to the audited accounts. The customer references must be verified and ready.

The prepared seller runs the process like a professional seller, and the professional standard converts directly into price. The buyer pays for certainty, and the seller who provides it captures the premium.

## Summary

The exit process is an information contest, and vendor due diligence is the seller's weapon. A prepared data room preempts the buyer's findings, shifts the multiple, and shortens the process. The package must be built before the process starts, to a standard the buyer's advisors will test. The seller who controls the information controls the price.
